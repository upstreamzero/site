## Research question

Is the evaluator's stated importance ranking calibrated to what actually
moves its recommendations?

## Why it mattered

The extraction reports which requirements matter. This run tested whether the
stated ranking predicts which requirements actually move the output when
flipped.

## Method

Evaluator: ChatGPT, logged out, incognito. Session separation was a hard
rule: Session A harvested the predicted-importance ranking (banked free from
E-007), and Session B ran the flips in fresh sessions so the model could not
honor its own earlier narration. Flip 1 changed a high-predicted variable;
Flip 2 changed a low-predicted variable. Sample: one draw per flip.

## Direct observations

- The predicted-most-important variable produced category dissolution.
- The predicted-least-important variable produced a leader swap.

## Interpretation (not established)

Ordinal calibration is supported; absolute calibration is indeterminate. This
is the first instance of a new measurement legitimately re-scoring a prior
interpretation without changing a single observation, which is a property
that distinguishes a measurement system from a narrative.

## Outcome and remaining uncertainty

Supported, for ordinal calibration only, and with a correction. The original
reading was that a low-ranked variable moved the recommendation. After E-009
established the baseline variance band, that leader swap was reclassified as
falling within the measured band. Per the coding record, Flip 2 no longer
provides strong evidence that a low-ranked variable meaningfully moved the
output. The claim was weakened by the measured variance, not resolved, which
would falsely assert evidence of no effect.

## Evidence and methods limitations

Raw outputs for this run are unrecoverable; only the coding survives. One
draw per flip, single coder. This is an instrument-calibration experiment,
not a validation.

## Contribution to the research program

prerequisite for magnitude work) were born here.
